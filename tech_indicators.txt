1. Overview
Name: Relative Strength Index (RSI)

Short Name: RSI (14)

Module: Technical

Category: Momentum

Purpose:
Measures the speed and magnitude of recent price movements to identify momentum strength and potential overbought or oversold conditions.

Created By:
J. Welles Wilder Jr.

Importance:
★★★★★ (Core Indicator)

Auto / Manual:
Auto

3. Industry Standard Calculation
Formula
Price Change = Current Close − Previous Close

Gain = Max(Change, 0)

Loss = Max(-Change, 0)

Average Gain = Wilder Smoothed Average

Average Loss = Wilder Smoothed Average

RS = Average Gain / Average Loss

RSI = 100 − (100 / (1 + RS))
Implementation Steps
1. Calculate the difference between consecutive closing prices.
2. Separate positive changes as Gains.
3. Separate negative changes as Losses.
4. Calculate the first Average Gain and Average Loss using a 14-period SMA.
5. From the next candle onward, use Wilder's smoothing:
   Avg Gain = ((Previous Avg Gain × 13) + Current Gain) / 14
   Avg Loss = ((Previous Avg Loss × 13) + Current Loss) / 14
6. Compute RS.
7. Compute RSI.
Notes
Use Wilder's original smoothing method.

Do not use a simple rolling average after initialization.

This matches TradingView, Bloomberg, and most institutional implementations.

Explain strong bullish momentum while warning that the market may be entering an overbought zone where short-term pullbacks become more likely.

Widely used by institutional and retail traders, making it a commonly watched market signal.

3. Industry Standard Calculation
Formula
Fast EMA = EMA(12)

Slow EMA = EMA(26)

MACD Line = Fast EMA − Slow EMA

Signal Line = EMA(9) of MACD Line

Histogram = MACD Line − Signal Line
Implementation Steps
1. Calculate EMA(12).

2. Calculate EMA(26).

3. Subtract EMA26 from EMA12.

4. Calculate EMA(9) of the MACD Line.

5. Compute Histogram.

6. Store all three values.
Notes
Use Exponential Moving Averages.

Signal Line must always be an EMA of the MACD Line.

Do not replace EMA with SMA.

Matches TradingView, Bloomberg and institutional platforms.

3. Industry Standard Calculation
Formula
Multiplier = 2 / (Period + 1)

EMA = (Current Close × Multiplier)
    + (Previous EMA × (1 − Multiplier))
For EMA(20):
Multiplier = 2 / (20 + 1)

Multiplier = 0.095238
Initialization
First EMA value = 20 Period Simple Moving Average (SMA)

Every subsequent value uses the EMA formula.
Implementation Steps
1. Collect 20 closing prices.

2. Calculate initial SMA.

3. Calculate multiplier.

4. Calculate next EMA using previous EMA.

5. Repeat for every completed candle.
Notes
Never initialize EMA using the first closing price.

Always initialize with the first 20-period SMA.

This matches TradingView, Bloomberg and institutional charting systems.

Why these weights?
These weights are based on the practical importance of technical indicators used by professional traders, institutional participants, and widely adopted trading platforms.
MACD, EMA 200, VWAP, Supertrend, and Technical Composite Score receive the highest weights because they provide strong confirmation of trend direction, institutional positioning, and overall market conditions. 
Market Regime has the highest confidence modifier (±10%) because it determines whether trend-following or mean-reversion strategies are appropriate, influencing the interpretation of nearly every other technical indicator. 
EMA 200, SMA 200, and India VIX receive higher confidence modifiers because they represent major long-term trend and risk signals that significantly affect market behavior. 
RSI, ADX, CMF, Fibonacci Retracement, Trendline, and Support/Resistance act as confirmation indicators, strengthening or weakening signals generated by the primary trend indicators. 
Williams %R, Pivot Points, TRIN, and Keltner Channel are shorter-term supporting indicators and therefore receive lower engine weights while still contributing valuable confirmation. 
Technical Composite Score carries the highest overall influence because it represents the aggregated output of the entire Technical module and serves as the primary technical input for the Decision Engine.

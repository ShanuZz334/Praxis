1. Overview
Name: PE Ratio
Short Name: PE
Module: Fundamentals
Category: Valuation
Purpose:
Measures how much investors are willing to pay for each unit of trailing earnings. It helps determine whether the market is trading at an attractive, fair, or expensive valuation relative to its historical range.
Based On:
Trailing Twelve Month (TTM) Earnings
Importance:
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Core Valuation Indicator)
Auto / Manual:
Manual (V1)
Update Frequency:
Whenever official valuation data is updated.
Praxis Note:
PE Ratio should always be interpreted relative to historical valuation levels. It should not generate buy or sell signals on its own and should be confirmed with growth and profitability indicators.

1. Overview
Name: Forward PE
Short Name: FWD PE
Module: Fundamentals
Category: Valuation
Purpose:
Measures the market valuation based on projected future earnings rather than historical earnings. It helps estimate whether current market expectations are optimistic, reasonable, or expensive.

5. EPS Growth
Module: Fundamentals
Category: Growth
Status: Production Ready
1. Overview
Name: Earnings Per Share Growth
Short Name: EPS Growth
Module: Fundamentals
Category: Growth
Purpose:
Measures the percentage growth in Earnings Per Share (EPS) over the previous reporting period. It indicates whether the market's earnings are improving or deteriorating over time.
Based On:
Reported Earnings Per Share (TTM / Annual)
Importance:
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Core Growth Indicator)
Auto / Manual:
Manual (V1)
Update Frequency:
After every quarterly earnings season or official EPS update.
Praxis Note:
EPS Growth is one of the strongest indicators of fundamental improvement. Consistent positive EPS growth generally supports higher market valuations, while declining EPS growth often signals weakening fundamentals.

Formula
EPS Growth (%) =

Compare against historical growth trends. 
Generate the Growth Score. 
Notes

Quarterly comparisons should use the same quarter from the previous year. 
Exclude one-time extraordinary earnings where possible.

Why these weights?
These weights are based on how institutional investors typically evaluate companies and markets:
PE Ratio, Forward PE, EPS Growth, ROE, and ROCE receive the highest weights because they are the most influential valuation and quality metrics. 
Debt to Equity is the most important financial risk metric, so it receives the highest weight within Financial Health. 
Market Cap to GDP is the strongest macro valuation indicator, while FII/DII Flow is the strongest sentiment indicator within Market Health. 
Current Ratio and Dividend Yield receive lower weights because, although useful, they are generally supporting indicators rather than primary drivers of long-term investment decisions.

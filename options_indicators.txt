3. Open Interest Change (OI Change)
Module: Options
Category: Open Interest
Status: Production Ready
1. Overview
Name: Open Interest Change
Short Name: OI Change
Module: Options
Category: Open Interest
Purpose:
Measures the change in Open Interest between consecutive trading sessions or option chain updates. It helps identify whether new positions are being created or existing positions are being closed, providing insight into market participation and trend strength.
Based On:
Current Open Interest and Previous Open Interest
Importance:
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Core Positioning Indicator)
Auto / Manual:
Auto
Update Frequency:
Every Option Chain Refresh (Market Hours)
Praxis Note:
OI Change is most effective when analyzed together with price movement. Rising OI with rising prices generally indicates Long Build-up, while rising OI with falling prices indicates Short Build-up.

2. Data Requirements
Required Data
Current Open Interest 
Previous Open Interest 
Current Spot Price 
Previous Spot Price 
Expiry Date 
Minimum Historical Data
5 Trading Days
Recommended
30 Trading Days
Primary Source
NSE Option Chain 
Upstox API 

3. Industry Standard Calculation
Formula
OI Change =
Current Open Interest − Previous Open Interest
OI Change (%) =
((Current OI − Previous OI) ÷ Previous OI) × 100
Implementation Steps
Retrieve the latest option chain. 
Compare current OI with previous OI. 
Calculate absolute and percentage change. 
Compare OI movement with price movement. 
Classify market positioning. 
Generate the Options Score. 
OI Interpretation
Notes
Use both absolute and percentage OI Change. 
Compare against previous completed update. 
Use spot price for classification.

Why these weights?
These weights are based on the relative importance of options indicators used by professional derivatives traders, institutional participants, and NSE option chain analysis.
Open Interest Change (14%) receives the highest weight because it reveals whether fresh money is entering or exiting the market, making it the strongest indicator of institutional positioning. 
PCR (Open Interest) (13%) is the primary sentiment indicator and is widely used to identify bullish, bearish, and contrarian positioning. 
ATM Implied Volatility (10%) is the foundation of volatility analysis and directly influences option pricing and risk. 
Max Pain (10%) becomes increasingly valuable during expiry and provides insight into institutional expiry positioning. 
Delta (8%) is the most important Greek, measuring directional exposure and option responsiveness to underlying price movement. 
Total Call OI (8%) and Total Put OI (8%) identify major resistance and support zones created by option writers. 
IV Rank (6%) provides historical context for current implied volatility, while PCR (Volume) (7%) offers faster but less reliable sentiment confirmation. 
Gamma, Theta, Vega, and IV Percentile (4% each) serve as supporting indicators that refine risk assessment and option pricing without dominating the overall Options Score.

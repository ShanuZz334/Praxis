Weighting Rationale
The Global module is designed around the factors that have the greatest influence on Indian equity markets rather than simply global importance.
S&P 500 Futures (14%) receives the highest weight because it is the strongest overnight indicator for NIFTY and reflects broad U.S. market sentiment. 
US 10-Year Treasury Yield (15%) carries the single highest individual weight, as global interest rates directly influence liquidity, equity valuations, and foreign capital flows. 
USD/INR (12%) is highly weighted due to its immediate impact on inflation, import costs, and FII/FPI sentiment in India. 
CBOE VIX (10%) is the primary global risk indicator and significantly affects overall market confidence. 
Brent Crude Oil (10%) has a major influence on India's economy because of the country's dependence on crude oil imports. 
Nasdaq Futures (10%) provide strong confirmation of global technology and growth sentiment, especially relevant for India's IT sector. 
US Dollar Index (DXY) (8%) measures global Dollar strength and complements USD/INR in assessing capital flow and liquidity conditions. 
Gold (6%) serves as the primary safe-haven asset and helps identify shifts toward risk-off sentiment. 
Dow Jones Futures (6%) provide confirmation of institutional confidence in large-cap U.S. equities. 
Bitcoin (5%) contributes as a supplementary indicator of speculative risk appetite but should never dominate the Global Score. 
Silver (4%) acts as a supporting industrial commodity indicator with the lowest weighting due to its relatively indirect impact on Indian equities. 
This weighting structure ensures that global equity markets, interest rates, currencies, and macroeconomic risk drive the Global Score, while commodities and digital assets provide confirmation rather than dominating the Decision Engine. It aligns the Global module with institutional market practices and the specific macroeconomic sensitivities of the Indian market.
